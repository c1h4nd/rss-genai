# European Intellectual History since Nietzsche: Class 20, Violence and the Sacred: René Girard

<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc>

Prof. Marcy Shore’s Yale Courses lecture, titled **"European Intellectual History since Nietzsche: Class 20, Violence and the Sacred: René Girard"**, covers René Girard’s philosophical and anthropological theories, comparing his framework to Claude Lévi-Strauss and structuralism.

### 1. Context & Personal Background

- **Girard’s Circle at Stanford [[01:00](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=60)]:** The professor recalls studying under René Girard at Stanford during the 1990s, highlighting the pre-internet, highly intensive, almost cult-like atmosphere around his seminars.
- **Philosophical Tradition:** Girard’s ideas are introduced as a dialogue with Claude Lévi-Strauss and Structuralism, offering a system of thought that purports to explain totality [[06:15](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=375)].

### 2. Structuralism & Claude Lévi-Strauss

- **Relational Meaning [[03:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=187)]:** Meaning is not intrinsic or imminent; it exists purely in relation to other signifiers within a stable, synchronic structure.
- **Crisis of Differentiation [[11:05](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=665)]:**

  - Myths begin with a *crisis of differentiation*—a breakdown or blurring of societal distinctions (e.g., sacred/profane, mortal/god, social hierarchies).
  - Contrary to intuition, society enters a crisis not when there is too much difference, but when boundaries collapse [[12:17](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=737)].
- **Decentering the Subject [[15:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=907)]:** Agency belongs to the structure/grammar itself rather than the individual human subject ("myths think themselves through people").

### 3. Girard’s Critique of Lévi-Strauss

- **Reaching the *Ding an sich* [[17:09](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1029)]:** Girard rejects Lévi-Strauss’s purely formal, semiotic approach, arguing that structuralism stays on the surface of signifiers playing with other signifiers.
- **Underneath the Structure [[19:06](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1146)]:** Girard seeks to penetrate beneath the signifiers to uncover real human substance, actual historical suffering, and violent sacrifice.

### 4. Part I: Mimetic Theory (İmmitasyon Modeli)

- **Mimetic Desire [[20:53](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1253)]:** Desire is not autonomous or direct. A subject (SSS) desires an object (OOO) because it mimics/imitates the desire of a Model/Mediator (MMM).
- **Mimetic Escalation [[25:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1539)]:**

  1. *Mimetic Desire* (desiring what the other desires)
  2. *Mimetic Rivalry* (competing for finite/limited objects)
  3. *Mimetic Violence* (uninhibited conflict)
- **Internal vs. External Mediation [[30:49](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1849)]:**

  - *External Mediation:* Large social or categorical distance between Subject and Mediator prevents direct conflict.
  - *Internal Mediation:* When social distinctions breakdown, Subject and Mediator become direct rivals, escalating into reciprocal violence [[32:51](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1971)].
- **The Scandal [[29:54](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=1794)]:** The Mediator is simultaneously admired as a model and hated as an obstacle.

### 5. Part II: The Scapegoat & The Sacred (Kurban ve Kutsal Modeli)

- **The Scapegoat Mechanism (Surrogate Victim) [[34:57](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2097)]:**

  - Uninhibited mimetic violence is resolved only through the spontaneous expulsion or murder of a *scapegoat* (surrogate victim).
  - All conflicting parties unite their shared rage against this single victim, restoring social peace and order [[35:39](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2139)].
- **Double Transference & *Pharmakos* [[41:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2495)]:**

  - The mob projects its own internal chaos onto the scapegoat, viewing the victim as both the source of the disorder (*poison*) and the source of the restored peace (*remedy*).
  - This double transference elevates the victim into a mystical, divine role (*The Sacred*) [[42:12](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2532)].
- **Three Cultural Pillars [[45:03](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2703)]:**

  1. **Prohibitions:** Rules designed to preserve differentiations and prevent mimetic conflict [[45:13](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2713)].
  2. **Ritual:** Controlled reenactments of the original sacrificial event to reinforce social order [[45:56](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2756)].
  3. **Myth:** The story of the scapegoat’s murder, **told strictly from the perspective of the persecutors/killers** to justify the violence [[47:35](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2855)].

### 6. Truth, Victims, and Christianity

- **Differentiating Modern Anxieties [[48:07](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2887)]:** The *crisis of differentiation* framework helps explain historical and social persecutions (e.g., anti-Semitism in assimilated 1930s Germany or modern cultural anxieties around blurred gender roles) [[48:55](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2935)].
- **The Reality Reference [[49:31](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=2971)]:** The truth of any myth can only be uncovered by deconstructing it and viewing the events from the perspective of the **victim**.
- **Christian Revelation [[50:19](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=3019)]:** Girard argues that Christianity breaks the mythological cycle by telling the story of Jesus Christ's murder from the perspective of the innocent victim rather than the persecuting mob [[50:41](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PTz65ixvjjc&t=3041)].
